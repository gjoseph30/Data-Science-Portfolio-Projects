# The Rising Cost of Infant Care: A State-by-State Analysis

## Overview

This project analyzes infant childcare costs across the United States using the National Database of Childcare Prices from 2008–2018.

The analysis examines national cost trends, differences between home-based and center-based infant care, geographic variation among states, and changes in childcare costs over time.

An interactive Power BI dashboard was developed to communicate the findings through national trends, state comparisons, cost gaps, and user-controlled filters.

## Project Objective

How have infant childcare costs changed across the United States, and how do costs differ by location and type of care?

## Data

The analysis uses childcare price data covering the period from 2008 through 2018.

The project focuses on:

- Home-based infant care costs
- Center-based infant care costs
- State-level differences
- National cost trends
- Cost growth over time
- Differences between care models

## Power BI Dashboard

The interactive dashboard was designed to allow users to explore infant childcare costs by state and year.

Dashboard components include:

- National infant care cost trends from 2008–2018
- Home-based versus center-based care comparison
- States with the highest center-based infant care costs
- States with the largest cost differences between care models
- Infant care cost growth from 2008–2018
- State and year filters

## Key Findings

Center-based infant care was consistently more expensive than home-based care throughout the period analyzed.

Nationally, both home-based and center-based infant care costs increased by more than 20% between 2008 and 2018, indicating increasing affordability pressure for families.

Substantial geographic differences were also observed. States including Hawaii, California, Washington, and Massachusetts were among those with comparatively high childcare costs.

In some states, center-based infant care exceeded home-based care by more than $100 per week, demonstrating significant differences between care models.

## Data Story

The analysis communicates three primary findings:

1. Infant childcare costs increased consistently over time.
2. Center-based care maintained a persistent cost premium over home-based care.
3. Geographic location strongly influenced the amount families paid for infant care.

Together, these findings demonstrate that childcare affordability is both a national and geographic issue.

## Limitations and Future Analysis

The analysis focuses primarily on childcare prices and does not directly account for differences in household income, inflation-adjusted purchasing power, government subsidies, or urban-rural variation.

Future analysis could integrate household income, regional economic indicators, childcare policies, and workforce wage data to provide additional context for affordability differences.
